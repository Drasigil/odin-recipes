Description

A type of dumpling dish, typically created using semolina (or plain wheat flour), mashed potato, and egg. Scrumptuous and flavorful, with a wide variety of options for unique spins and variations. Often recognized by its distinctive ridge, which are purpose-made to hold more sauce. Smooth variations also exist.

History of the dish

The modern gnocchi originate from Norther Italy, whose climate is well-suited for the successful cultivation of potato crops. This is especially important, as good-quality grain was consequently harder to grow, for the same reason, leading to the potato's gradual incorporation into Italian cuisine. Before the relatively recent adoption of the potato, gnocchi was traditionally made using squash and breadcrumbs, two staples of the peasant diet at the time. Unsurprisingly, gnocchi was considered cucina povera, or "poor cuisine."

Interesting tidbit

The name gnocchi means "lumps" in Italian.

Recipe

Ingredients Needed

  • Potatoes (Yukon Gold or Russet) - 3 lbs
  • Organic all-purpose flour (1-2 cups)
  • Egg (1)
  • Fine sea salt (1 tsp)

Steps

  1. Pre-heat oven to 450 F
    1. Scrub potatoes under running water while oven heats
    2. Poke holes in each potato with fork
    3. Place baking pan at the bottom of the oven to catch drippings
    4. Place potatoes on middle rack of oven
  2. Cook for 45-50 minutes
    1. Test for doneness by wearing an oven mitt and gently squeezing a potato
    2. They are fully cooked when you feel a slight give under the mitt
  3. Remove potatoes from the oven and turn off the oven
    1. Allow the potatoes to cool for about 15-30 minutes
    2. Potatoes should be warm, but handleable
  4. Peel the potatoes with your fingers
    1. Place the peelings into a medium-sized bowl
    2. (Optional) Place bowl with peelings aside for later to use with potato skin recipes
    3. Place remainder of potatoes in a large bowl or on the baking pan previously used to catch drippings
  5. Set up a large, clean work surface
    1. Dust the space with flour
    2. Use a potato ricer to mask all of the still-warm potatoes onto the work surface
    3. Form a well in the center of the mash
  6. Beat the egg with a teaspoon of fine sea salt, then pour it into the potato well
    1. Use about 1/2 a cupe of flour at a time and begin to incrementally mix it with the potato and egg
    2. Gently mix and continue to add more flour until the dough feels flexible and soft, but not sticky
    3. Form a ball of dough
  7. Knead the dough-ball for 3-4 minutes until texture is smooth and consistent
  8. Divide the dough into even pieces or wedges, rolling each into a ball
  9. Starting with the first ball, roll it gently into a long log approximately 3/4 inch in diameter
    1. Cut the log into 1-inch pieces
    2. Use a fork (or your fingers) to roll down the back of each segment to create the signature ridges
  10. Gently place the now-shaped gnocchi onto a flat, parchment-lined baking dish
    1. Continue making the rest of the gnocchi
  11. Either cook gnocchi in a pot of boiling water (without salt) and serve with sauce, OR cover the gnocchi and refrigerate for up to 1 day before cooking
    • Alternatively: Freeze gnocchi, then cook them in the boiling water after removing them from the freezer
