Description

An Italian pasta dish consisting of fettuccine noodles tossed with butter and parmesan cheese, which has been melted and emuslified to create a rich, thick cream sauce.

History of the dish

Modern Fettuccine Alfredo originated in Rome in the early 20th century. It was created by Alfredo di Lelio as a nutritious, easy-to-eat meal for his ailing wife, who was weak after giving birth to her son in 1908. It was created with only 3 ingredients: fettuccine, Parmesan cheese, and butter.

When the restaurant di Lelio had been working in since 1892--Piazza Rosa--was closed to be condemned and destroyed, di Lelio opened his own restaurant called Alfredo alla Scrofa. It was here he would serve his newfound signature dish with a golden spook and fork, insribed "To Alfredo the King of the noodles." As time went on, he would later sell the restaurant to two of his employees.

Alfredo di Lelio, alongside his son, opened another restaurant in 1950, called Piazza Augusto Imperatore. His Alfredo dish became so widel known that di Lelio received invites to demonstrate it both inside and outside of Italy. The dish was called maestosissime fettuccine all'Alfredo, which means "most majestic Alfredo-style fettuccine." From there, the rest is history.

Interesting tidbit

When originally created, there was speculation about a secret third ingredient, such as oil in the pasta dough, or pasta cooked in milk.
